## MOOSE Improvements

### Matrix-free Kokkos Jacobian action

Setting `use_kokkos_matrix_free_jacobian = true` in the `Executioner` block makes a Kokkos solver
system compute the action of its Jacobian on a vector analytically, by partial assembly, instead of
assembling the Jacobian into a matrix. The operator serves as both SNES's `Amat` and `Pmat`, and it
supplies a matrix-vector product and a diagonal, so preconditioning is limited to what those two
operations support: `none`, `jacobi`, `pbjacobi`, `mg` and `shell`. The parameter requires a
Kokkos-enabled build, and cannot be combined with the `PJFNK` or `JFNK` solve types, which already
provide their own finite-difference matrix-free operator.

Kernels participate through the new `KernelValue` and `KernelGrad` interfaces, which ask a kernel for
its quadrature-point linearization once per Newton step rather than for element Jacobian entries.

!alert warning title=Interface change for Kokkos kernels
Kokkos kernels derived from `KokkosDiffusion`, and any kernel written against the value/flux form,
now override `precomputeQpResidual` and `precomputeQpJacobian` in place of `computeQpResidual` and
`computeQpJacobian`. The new signatures drop the test-function index and return the value or flux
rather than the contracted residual. Downstream Kokkos kernels overriding the old names will not
compile until they are updated.

### p-multigrid preconditioner

[PMultigrid.md] (`type = PMG`) preconditions the matrix-free Kokkos operator with a polynomial-order
multigrid hierarchy. Each coarse level holds the fine system's variables at a reduced order, on the
fine mesh and at the fine quadrature rule, so every level's operator is the same quadrature-point
linearization contracted against that level's own basis; a coarse level carries no kernels or
boundary conditions of its own. Levels are joined by a transfer that restricts by the transpose of
its prolongation, which makes each level the Galerkin operator of the fine linearization.

The default smoother inverts the block of degrees of freedom each mesh entity carries rather than
reading the operator diagonal, which reaches the modes a point smoother cannot damp at high
polynomial order. Both the operator and the cycle are symmetric for a steady system, so CG and MINRES
are valid outer accelerators.

### Essential boundary conditions projected by libMesh

[LibmeshDirichletBC.md] and [FunctionLibmeshDirichletBC.md] impose an essential boundary condition
whose prescribed value comes from libMesh's Dirichlet constraint machinery, which projects it onto the
variable's boundary trace space. Unlike `DirichletBC`, which assigns the value to one degree of
freedom per node as though that degree of freedom were a point value of the solution, these constrain
every degree of freedom the boundary reaches. That distinction matters on a modal basis such as
`HIERARCHIC` above first order, where a degree of freedom at a node is a bubble coefficient and a node
may carry several of them, and it is why the `hp` adaptivity tests now use the projected form.

### Complete removal of using-directives for namespace libMesh

Using-directives for namespace libMesh, i.e., `using namespace libMesh;`, have been entirely
removed. Prefer using qualified names, e.g., `libMesh::VectorValue` for `VectorValue`. If you feel
that practice becomes prohibitively verbose, cumbersome or hampers readability, leverage
using-declarations for libMesh namespace members, e.g. `using libMesh::VectorValue;`. Unity builds
will leak using-declarations across translation units within a folder, so be mindful. This is also the reason
using-directives have been removed: they leak the entire namespace across translation units.
