# Vickers Indentation with an STL Contactor

## Description

This example exercises the full rigid-body contact stack:

- Contactor described by an *arbitrary* triangulated surface
  ([SurfaceMeshContactor.md] reading an STL file), rather than an
  analytic sphere.
- Finite-strain J2 plasticity in the deformable body.
- Force-controlled loading via the load-control layer.
- Outer [UzawaTransient.md] driving a scalar Newton on the indenter
  depth, wrapping an inner primal solve on $(u, \lambda)$.

The deformable body is a cube read from `contact_test.e`; its top face
carries the contact sideset `mat_top`.  The mesh's own rigid-indenter
block is stripped and replaced by a Vickers pyramid loaded from
`unit_sphere.stl` (actually a unit-scaled Vickers geometry --- see the
input) scaled to $2\times$ and translated to $(0, 0.04, 0)$.  The
target reaction is a linear ramp to $1.5\times 10^5$; the material
follows a J2 power-law hardening curve consistent with the other
plastic examples.

## Why this example needs the whole stack

- The STL contactor exercises the angle-weighted pseudonormal
  discussed in the [theory page](modules/contact/rigid_contact/theory.md#level-set-contactors).
  Corners of the deformable-body sideset that project onto a vertex of
  the STL pyramid would otherwise take a wrong sign for the reported
  gap; the pseudonormal keeps the signed distance correct.
- The plastic response evolves quickly, so a single constant
  preconditioning shift on the load-control diagonal is a
  compromise --- large enough during initial impact tends to slow
  convergence once the plastic patch is established, and vice versa.
  [RigidBodyLoadControl.md] accepts a function-of-time variant of the
  shift through
  [!param](/ScalarKernels/RigidBodyLoadControl/kss_stiffness_function)
  for exactly this situation; the shipped input uses a constant
  ($10^6$) as a reasonable starting point.
- Monolithic Newton on the coupled $(u, \lambda, s)$ system stalls
  on the pathological $R_s$ direction late in the ramp.  The
  [UzawaTransient.md] executioner sidesteps this by splitting the
  solve into an outer scalar Newton on $s$ and an inner primal
  solve on $(u, \lambda)$ at fixed $s$.

## Solver

The executioner is [UzawaTransient.md] with a warm-start
[RigidBodyContactPredictor.md]:

!listing modules/contact/examples/rigid/stl-contact/material_into_indenter.i block=Executioner

Key knobs:

- `load_control_kernel` names the [RigidBodyLoadControl.md] scalar
  kernel (`rigid_contact_load_control_top`, generated by the action)
  that the outer Uzawa loop drives.
- `outer_max_iter`, `outer_abs_tol`, `outer_rel_tol`, `max_step`
  govern the 1D outer Newton on $s$.
- The inner primal solve is plain Newton + LU, with `automatic_scaling
  = false` so the `kss_stiffness` shift on the scalar row is not
  renormalized away.
- The `[Predictor]` sub-block emits a warm-start
  [RigidBodyContactPredictor.md] with $k=3$ hops into the bulk.
