# PorousFlowAquiferBC

!syntax description /BCs/PorousFlowAquiferBC

`PorousFlowAquiferBC` applies a Robin (Cauchy) boundary condition that couples the model
boundary to a far-field aquifer.  The mass flux leaving the domain is proportional to the
difference between the model pore pressure and the aquifer pressure evaluated at the same
elevation:
\begin{equation}
  \label{eq:aquifer_flux}
  s = C \left( P_{\mathrm{model}} - P_{\mathrm{aq}}(z) \right) ,
\end{equation}
where $C$ is the conductance (kg.m$^{-2}$.Pa$^{-1}$.s$^{-1}$), $P_{\mathrm{model}}$ is the
nodal pore pressure, and $z$ is the elevation at the current quadrature point.  A positive
value of $s$ means fluid is leaving the domain (the boundary acts as a sink); a negative
value means fluid is entering (a source).

The elevation $z$ is computed per quadrature point from the position vector $\mathbf{x}$ and
the user-supplied gravity vector $\mathbf{g}$:
\begin{equation}
  z = - \mathbf{x} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{g}} ,
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mathbf{g}} = \mathbf{g}/|\mathbf{g}|$ is the unit vector in the direction of
gravity (e.g. for gravity acting in $-z$: $\hat{\mathbf{g}} = (0,0,-1)$, so $z =
\mathbf{x}\cdot(0,0,1)$, which is the $z$-coordinate).  Because $z$ is re-evaluated at every
quadrature point, the hydrostatic correction is correct on boundaries of any orientation,
including vertical faces.

The fluid density used in the hydrostatic correction, $\rho_{\mathrm{nodal}}$, is taken from
PorousFlow's own nodal material property `PorousFlow_fluid_phase_density_nodal`.  This makes
the aquifer pressure self-consistent with the equation of state used in the rest of the model.

## Reference-pressure formulations

Exactly one of the following two parameters must be supplied to define the far-field aquifer
pressure.

### Hydraulic head formulation (`aquifer_head`)

\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{aq}}(z) = \rho_{\mathrm{nodal}} \, |\mathbf{g}| \, (h_{\mathrm{aq}} - z) ,
\end{equation}
where $h_{\mathrm{aq}}$ is the far-field hydraulic head (m).  This form has the important
property that the flux is identically zero at every point on a boundary whose model pressure
is at hydrostatic equilibrium with the aquifer, even when the boundary has vertical extent.
This is because the hydraulic head $h = z + P/(\rho g)$ is constant throughout a hydrostatic
column, so $P_{\mathrm{model}}(z) = \rho g (h - z) = P_{\mathrm{aq}}(z)$ everywhere.

The conductance $C$ must be supplied directly via `aquifer_conductance` (kg.m$^{-2}$.Pa$^{-1}$.s$^{-1}$).  A physically reasonable estimate is $C = \rho k / (\mu L)$, where $k$ is the
permeability of the intervening material (m$^2$), $\mu$ is the fluid viscosity (Pa.s), and
$L$ is the distance to the far-field aquifer (m).

### Pressure-at-datum formulation (`aquifer_pressure_at_datum`)

\begin{equation}
  P_{\mathrm{aq}}(z) = P_{\mathrm{datum}} + \rho_{\mathrm{nodal}} \, |\mathbf{g}| \,
  (z_{\mathrm{datum}} - z) ,
\end{equation}
where $P_{\mathrm{datum}}$ is the aquifer pressure at reference elevation $z_{\mathrm{datum}}$.
This form is convenient when the aquifer pressure at a specific depth is known from field data.

For this formulation the conductance is computed automatically from the permeability and
viscosity material properties:
\begin{equation}
  C = \frac{\rho_{\mathrm{nodal}} \, k_{nn}}{\mu \, L} ,
\end{equation}
where $k_{nn}$ is the permeability of the material between the boundary and the far-field
aquifer, $\mu$ is the nodal fluid viscosity, and $L$ is the distance to the far-field aquifer
supplied via `aquifer_distance` (m).

By default $k_{nn} = (\mathbf{K}\mathbf{n})\cdot\mathbf{n}$ is the boundary permeability
material property projected onto the outward boundary normal.  This is appropriate in the
common case where the mesh truncates a laterally extensive formation and the aquifer is a
continuation of the boundary material; heterogeneity along the boundary and anisotropy are
then handled automatically.  When the intervening material differs from the boundary material
(for example a low-permeability reservoir edge connected to a regional aquifer through
higher-permeability strata), the optional `aquifer_permeability` parameter (m$^2$) overrides
the boundary value.

## Effect of aquifer distance

For the pressure-at-datum formulation the single parameter $L$ controls the strength of the
hydraulic coupling.  For the head formulation an equivalent distance can be recovered from the
supplied conductance as $L = \rho k / (\mu C)$.  In either case:

- $L \to 0$: $C \to \infty$.  An arbitrarily large flux is generated by any nonzero
  pressure difference, driving $P_{\mathrm{model}} \to P_{\mathrm{aq}}$.  The boundary
  approaches a Dirichlet condition pinned to the aquifer pressure.

- $L \to \infty$: $C \to 0$.  The flux approaches zero regardless of the pressure
  difference.  The boundary approaches an impermeable (Neumann) condition.

The equilibration time constant for a compressible domain of length $L_{\mathrm{domain}}$,
porosity $\phi$, fluid bulk modulus $K_f$, permeability $k$, and viscosity $\mu$ is
approximately
\begin{equation}
  \tau \approx \frac{\phi \, \mu \, L_{\mathrm{domain}} \, L}{k \, K_f} ,
\end{equation}
so $L$ can be chosen to match a desired response timescale.

## Comparison with `PorousFlowPiecewiseLinearSink`

[`PorousFlowPiecewiseLinearSink`](PorousFlowPiecewiseLinearSink.md) with `pt_vals`/`multipliers`
encoding $g(x)=x$, `flux_function = C`, and `PT_shift = Pe` applies
\begin{equation}
  s = C \, (P - P_e) ,
\end{equation}
where $P_e$ is a single scalar.  This is equivalent to `PorousFlowAquiferBC` only when the
boundary is horizontal (constant elevation) and the density is approximately constant, so that
$P_e = \rho_0 |\mathbf{g}| (h_{\mathrm{aq}} - z_0)$ can be pre-computed at a single elevation
$z_0$.

On a vertical or inclined boundary $P_{\mathrm{aq}}$ varies with elevation and a single
scalar `PT_shift` matches the aquifer pressure at only one height.  `PorousFlowAquiferBC`
evaluates $z$ and $P_{\mathrm{aq}}(z)$ per quadrature point, so it is correct on boundaries
of any orientation.

A further difference is that `PorousFlowAquiferBC` uses $\rho_{\mathrm{nodal}}$ from
PorousFlow's own equation of state in the hydrostatic correction, so the aquifer pressure is
consistent with the model fluid properties.  A `PorousFlowPiecewiseLinearSink` with a
pre-computed scalar `PT_shift` uses a fixed reference density, which introduces a small error
for compressible fluids.

See [boundary conditions](boundaries.md) for more details and examples.
